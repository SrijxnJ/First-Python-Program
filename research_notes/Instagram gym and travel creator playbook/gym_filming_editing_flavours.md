## KQ1. Edit "flavours" for gym content — when to use, pacing, transitions, speed ramps, text, sound, grade, music

### Takeaway
2026 gym Reels cluster into two macro-families: (a) music-driven montage edits (phonk/"montagem" velocity edits, cinematic/moody, aesthetic slow-mo, physique check) where speed ramps synced to beat markers do the storytelling, and (b) talk-driven formats (form-tip, raw vlog, day-in-the-life, POV skit, voice-over "diary") where a spoken or on-screen hook in the first 3 seconds, word-by-word captions and dead-air removal matter more than effects. Hook-fatigue research for 2026 says "POV: you finally…", before/after openers and countdown openers are now underperforming, while "stall", "reframe" and "contrarian" hooks drive reach.

### Cited Findings

**Hooks, pacing and text (talk-driven flavours)**
- Fitness content in 2026 faces "transformation fatigue"; hooks that name a specific failure state or contradict repeated niche advice outperform hooks describing the goal state — [BlitzCut, Fitness & Mindset Reel Hooks 2026](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/fitness-mindset-reel-hooks-2026)
- Eight hook types with templates: Stall ("If you've been in a deficit for eight weeks and the scale hasn't moved…"), Reframe ("You're not unmotivated. Your plan requires motivation, which is a design flaw."), Contrarian ("Progressive overload is not your problem if you're sleeping six hours."), Cost-of-Delay, Credential-Free Proof ("I added 40 pounds to my squat in a year training three days weekly with two kids."), Diagnostic ("If you can't hold a bodyweight squat for 60 seconds, your mobility work isn't the fix."), Small-Number ("Two minutes. That's the whole warm-up…"), Mid-Confession — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/fitness-mindset-reel-hooks-2026)
- Hook must resolve within 3 seconds, one sentence max; put 5–8 words of on-screen text in the first frame (muted autoplay); animated word-by-word captions outperform static text cards for cold reach; remove silence between sentences; introduce yourself *after* the hook; use transformation footage as mid-reel proof, not the opener — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/fitness-mindset-reel-hooks-2026)
- Hook types by goal: Shares → Reframe/Stall; Saves → Diagnostic/Small-Number; Comments → Contrarian/Mid-Confession; Completion → Cost-of-Delay/Proof; Cold reach → Stall/Reframe — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/fitness-mindset-reel-hooks-2026)
- Hooks flagged as ineffective in 2026: "POV: you finally…" (format fatigue), before/after as opening (reads as an ad), "Save this" as hook, countdown openers ("5 tips for…"), abstract mindset statements — [BlitzCut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/fitness-mindset-reel-hooks-2026)
- Hooks should create curiosity, contradiction or shock in under 8 words — [PostIgniter 2026 Reels trends](https://postigniter.com/blog/instagram-caption-generator-for-reels-ai-prompts-that-match-2026-reels-trends)
- POV skits like "gym newbie vs. gym pro" are described as hugely popular and shareable because they show you understand your audience's struggles — [StackInfluence, Reels content ideas 2026](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/instagram-reels-content-ideas-influencers-2026)
- Aesthetic "day in my life" Reels with a soft feel are surging in 2026 among 18–34s; captions should read like a journal entry, not a press release — [InfluenceFlow, 100+ Reels ideas 2026](https://influenceflow.io/resources/100-instagram-reels-content-ideas-to-skyrocket-your-engagement-in-2026/)

**Velocity / phonk montage flavour (speed-ramp mechanics)**
- A velocity edit changes speed at different points, "usually involving speeding up certain parts and slowing down others, often synced to the beat of a song"; for gym edits, "apply a speed ramp on the lift or last rep" — [FlexClip, How to make a velocity edit on CapCut](https://flexclip.com/learn/how-to-make-a-velocity-edit-on-capcut.html)
- CapCut preset velocity curves: Montage, Hero, Bullet, Jump Cut, Flash In, Flash Out (Edit > Speed > Curve); drag a beat point up to speed up, down to slow down — [FlexClip](https://flexclip.com/learn/how-to-make-a-velocity-edit-on-capcut.html)
- Speed-ramp principle: isolate the slow section to the payoff moment, keep entry and exit faster than the middle, cut dead space right after the slowed moment — "Slow motion feels polished when it arrives late and leaves early." Beat-sync workflow: edit normally before the beat, drop into slow-mo just before/on the beat, land the key movement in the slowed section, return to normal speed once the payoff is clear — [Nemo Video, CapCut slow motion & speed ramp](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/capcut-slow-motion-speed-ramp)
- Phonk edits are described as trending for fitness creators because of "raw beats and electrifying energy"; phonk gym templates pair footage with "stylized visual effects that complement the dark and moody tones of Phonk music" and "seamless transitions that match the beat" — [Pippit / CapCut gym phonk templates](https://pippit.capcut.com/templates/gym-edits-template-phonk); [CapCut explore: gym edit x phonk](https://www.capcut.com/explore/gym-edit-x-phonk)

**Music by flavour (2025–2026)**
- Phonk in 2025 is defined by two trends — Aggressive Drift Phonk and Brazilian Phonk (Funk Phonk/"montagem"); MXZI and ATLXS are named as leading artists in streaming; Kordhell ("Murder in My Mind"; "MONTAGEM – PR FUNK (Remix)", July 2023) remains one of the most popular phonk artists; "AURA MONTAGEM" playlists bundle gym phonk/montagem edit songs popular on TikTok — [StudentBridge phonk 2025 overview](https://slbubble.studentbridge.com/celebnews231/boiii-what-u-say-about-phonk-the-5-viral-trends-top-artists-and-aggressive-sounds-dominating-2025-uzugm.html); [Qobuz Kordhell bio](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/interpreter/kordhell/10671468); [artist.tools top phonk playlists](https://www.artist.tools/top-playlists/phonk) *(note: the StudentBridge page is a low-authority aggregator; artist names are corroborated by the playlist listing)*

## KQ2. Per muscle day — exercises to film, tripod angle/height/distance, lens, framing, lighting, money shots, 15–30 s recipes

### Takeaway
Lifting-coach consensus is to film compound lifts from a rear-45° (squat), front-45° (deadlift) or dead-side angle at roughly hip height with the whole body and the close end of the bar in frame; social-media creators generally raise the camera to hip-to-chest height (140–165 cm) and favour the 45° angle because "it looks better on social media". Lighting that reveals muscle is side/window light, not the gym's overhead fluorescents.

### Cited Findings

**Angles and heights for the big lifts (coach/forum sources)**
- Squat: film from a rear 45° angle at roughly hip height — this "keeps the plates out of the way and allows you to see all of the joints"; Deadlift: front 45° angle, and "make sure to see the entire bar path, which means you need to see the close end of the bar for the entire range of the rep"; Bench: "usually shot best from above the head"; Overhead press: "front quarter, so you can see the lockout position and the entire body at least from the knees up" — [Starting Strength forum, posting technique videos](https://startingstrength.com/resources/forum/technique/20249-posting-technique-forum-post189358.html) *(via search summary; the page itself returned 403 on fetch)*
- Alternative view (Barbell Medicine forum): squat, deadlift and overhead press "directly side view, from the floor"; squat "side view, with the camera level with the hips at the bottom for a dead on look at depth"; the 45° angle is used "particularly on heavy lifts" because it "look[s] better on social media" — [Barbell Medicine forum, Camera angle for main lifts](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/camera-angle-for-main-lifts/7702)
- Creator guidance: camera between hip and chest height, not on the floor; "most creators land between 55–65 inches (140–165 cm) for standing angles"; pick a tripod that reaches target framing "without fully extending the center column"; ball heads are enough for aim-once-and-lock gym work — [GeekChamp tripod/angle guide](https://geekchamp.com/?p=281277)
- Coaching-video guidance: use frontal, side and overhead views; shoot "from a 45-degree or side angle so the form cues are visible"; close-ups for technique-intensive movements — [Coachway, how to film exercise videos](https://coachway.io/articles/how-to-film-exercise-videos-for-online-coaching/); [Kraftgeek, recording workout videos on your phone](https://kraftgeek.com/blogs/creator-inspiration/a-comprehensive-guide-to-recording-workout-videos-on-your-phone)

**Lighting**
- "Daylight from a window to your side and slightly above beats almost any set" of artificial lighting — [Fitune, film high-quality fitness videos with your phone](https://fitune.io/post/how-to-film-high-quality-fitness-videos-with-your-phone)
- Gym overhead fluorescents "cast unflattering shadows"; side-lighting "is better at revealing muscle striation and texture"; a slightly elevated camera angle looking down reads more "dominant and muscular", a lower angle makes you "appear taller and more imposing" — [GoodLife Fitness, perfect gym selfie](https://blog.goodlifefitness.com/article/how-to-take-the-perfect-gym-selfie); [Aaptiv gym photo tips](https://aaptiv.com/magazine/?p=18378)
- Position yourself facing the light source so face and body are lit; avoid glare/overexposure; manually control focus, exposure and white balance rather than auto — [Kraftgeek](https://kraftgeek.com/blogs/creator-inspiration/a-comprehensive-guide-to-recording-workout-videos-on-your-phone)

**iPhone 15 lenses available for these angles**
- iPhone 15: 48 MP Main 26 mm ƒ/1.6 with sensor-shift OIS; camera system offers "2x optical zoom in, 2x optical zoom out; 4x optical zoom range" (the 2x is a sensor crop of the 48 MP main, the 0.5x is the ultra-wide); digital zoom up to 6x in video; Action mode up to 2.8K at 60 fps — [Apple iPhone 15 tech specs (IN)](https://support.apple.com/en-in/111831)
- Action mode "uses the ultrawide camera at 60fps and crops heavily" — [GSMArena iPhone 15 Pro review](https://www.gsmarena.com/apple_iphone_15_pro-review-2620p6.php)

### Inferences (per-day shot list — my synthesis; tripod heights map the sourced "hip height"/"140–165 cm" guidance onto the Syvo WT3130's 41–133 cm range)
Lens key: 0.5x = ultra-wide (use only ≥ 2 m away or for Action mode/low ceilings; it distorts limbs near frame edges), 1x = default for everything, 2x = crop of the main sensor — fine in good light for pump/detail shots, grainier under dim gym light.

- **Chest day** — Film: flat/incline DB press (camera at bench height, 45° from the head end, 1x, ~1.5 m), cable flye (front-on at chest height, 1x, framing from waist up so the contraction fills the frame), dips (side, hip height). Money shot: slow-mo (240 fps) cable-flye squeeze with light coming from one side so the sternum line shows; a top-down 0.5x shot from the tripod at max 133 cm over a flat bench. Recipe (20 s, phonk): 0–2 s text hook on a static tripod wide; 2–8 s three 1-s cuts (rack, chalk, lie down) on beats; 8–13 s 240 fps last rep ramped 300% → 15% → 200% on the drop; 13–18 s cable-flye squeeze 2 s + dip 2 s; 18–20 s freeze on pump + date overlay.
- **Shoulder day** — Film: lateral raises (front-on, camera at shoulder height ≈ 125–133 cm, 1x, 1.2–1.5 m), overhead press (front-quarter, knees-up framing per Starting Strength), rear-delt flyes (from behind, chest height). Money shot: "delt cap under side light" — stand with a window or a single bright fixture to one side, camera slightly above shoulder height, lateral raise at the top position in 240 fps; 2x lens for a tight shoulder close-up if light is good. Recipe (15 s, aesthetic slow-mo): 0–3 s slow push-in (keyframe scale 100→110%) on the standing wide; 3–10 s 240 fps lateral raise at 30% speed; 10–14 s rear-delt row from behind; 14–15 s cut to black on the last beat.
- **Back day** — Film: deadlift (front-45°, hip height ≈ 90–100 cm, far enough to see the close end of the bar through the whole rep — typically 2–2.5 m), lat pulldown/pull-ups from behind (camera at mid-back height, 1x), barbell row from the side. Money shot: "lat spread from behind" — tripod behind you at upper-back height, overhead light slightly in front so the lats' edges catch rim light; pull-up top position in slow-mo from behind. Recipe (25 s, cinematic): 0–3 s text hook + chalk close-up (2x); 3–9 s deadlift set-up in 60 fps at 70%; 9–15 s lockout in 240 fps at 25% with music swell; 15–21 s pull-ups from behind, 2 reps at 50%; 21–25 s lat-spread hold 2 s + fade.
- **Leg day** — Film: squat (rear-45°, hip height, whole body + plates visible, 2–3 m; Action mode on 0.5x if you need the walk-out in frame), leg press from the side at platform height, Bulgarian split squats from the side, hack squat from the rear-45°. Money shot: quad sweep from a low front-45° at ~60–70 cm (lower tripod column) during a walking lunge; calf raise slow-mo from behind at calf height (41 cm minimum). Recipe (30 s, day-in-the-life style): 0–2 s timestamp; 2–8 s hyperlapse walk-in 500%; 8–16 s squat set in 1x hip-height, cut per rep on beat; 16–22 s leg press side slow-mo; 22–28 s lunge low-angle; 28–30 s collapse-on-floor freeze.
- **Arm day** — Film: preacher/incline curls (front-45°, camera at elbow height, 1x–2x, ~1 m), overhead triceps extension from the side, rope pushdown front-on. Money shot: bicep peak in 240 fps at the top of a curl with side light; triceps horseshoe from the rear-45° during a pushdown lockout. Recipe (15 s, POV/humour or velocity): 0–2 s "arm day = ego day" text; 2–10 s alternating 0.5-s cuts curl/pushdown on beat; 10–14 s 240 fps peak contraction 20% speed; 14–15 s flex to camera + flash transition.
- **Abs** — Film: hanging leg raises from the side (camera at waist height), cable crunches front-45°, ab-wheel from the side at floor level (41 cm). Money shot: top-down 0.5x from a 133 cm tripod over a mat for crunches/leg raises; "serratus" shot from the front-45° on hanging raises with light from the side. Recipe (20 s, educational): 0–3 s hook text "Your leg raises are hip flexor raises"; 3–9 s split screen wrong vs right at 50%; 9–15 s freeze + tracked arrow on pelvis tilt; 15–20 s correct reps + caption CTA.

## KQ3. iPhone 15 settings for gyms (4K 24/30/60, slo-mo 240, Action mode, exposure lock, HDR on/off, flicker and mixed lighting)

### Takeaway
iPhone 15 shoots 4K at 24/25/30/60 fps, 1080p slo-mo at 120/240 fps, Action mode up to 2.8K60 and Dolby Vision HDR up to 4K60; the practical gym configuration is 4K30 or 4K60 (flicker-safe under mains-frequency lighting), 1080p240 only for the money rep, Action mode only for moving shots, AE/AF lock by touch-and-hold, and HDR Video turned off if the editing app or playback washes colours — with the caveat that slo-mo at 120/240 fps is where fluorescent/LED flicker shows up worst.

### Cited Findings

**Capabilities (primary Apple spec)**
- "4K video recording at 24 fps, 25 fps, 30 fps or 60 fps"; "1080p HD video recording at 25 fps, 30 fps or 60 fps"; "Slo-mo video support for 1080p at 120 fps or 240 fps"; "Cinematic mode up to 4K HDR at 30 fps"; "Action mode up to 2.8K at 60 fps"; "HDR video recording with Dolby Vision up to 4K at 60 fps"; "Sensor-shift optical image stabilisation for video (Main)"; "Digital zoom up to 6x" — [Apple iPhone 15 tech specs](https://support.apple.com/en-in/111831)

**Setting paths (Apple user guide, via search excerpts)**
- Action Mode Lower Light: "go to Settings > Camera > Record Video, then turn on Action Mode Lower Light" — [Apple, Change video recording settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-video-recording-settings-iphc1827d32f/ios)
- HDR: "iPhone records video in Dolby Vision HDR… To turn off HDR video recording, go to Settings > Camera > Record Video, then turn off HDR Video or HDR (depending on your model)" — [Apple, Adjust HDR camera settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/adjust-hdr-camera-settings-iph2cafe2ebc/ios)
- AE/AF lock: "To lock your manual focus and exposure settings for upcoming shots, touch and hold the focus area until you see AE/AF Lock; tap the screen to unlock settings" — [Apple, Use iPhone camera tools to set up your shot](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-your-shot-iph3dc593597/ios). The Camera Control "Lock Focus and Exposure" option applies only to iPhones with the Camera Control button (iPhone 16 family), not iPhone 15 — [Apple, Use the Camera Control](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-the-camera-control-iph0c397b154/ios)
- Action mode runs on the ultra-wide at 60 fps with a heavy crop (hence 2.8K) and "delivers super smooth and incredibly stabilized video"; Enhanced Stabilization is on by default — [GSMArena iPhone 15 Pro review](https://www.gsmarena.com/apple_iphone_15_pro-review-2620p6.php)
- One creator's recommended iPhone 15 configuration (opinion piece): 4K60; HDR Video on ("huge improvement in quality"); Enhanced Stabilization on; Auto FPS off "to capture dark scenes accurately with reduced noise"; Action Mode Lower Light on; Lock Camera off; Lock White Balance off; Formats: High Efficiency (HEVC) — [Kartick, Which camera settings work best on the iPhone 15](https://kartick.substack.com/p/which-camera-settings-work-best-on)

**Slo-mo gotcha**
- An iPhone 15 user reported slo-mo capping at ~165 fps instead of 240; replies attributed it to "low light, scene complexity, stabilization, or device limitations" — the iPhone dynamically lowers frame rate in dim scenes; no settings fix was identified — [Apple Community thread 255919244](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255919244)

**Flicker and mixed lighting**
- Fluorescent fixtures "commonly vary at 100 or 120 Hz" (2× mains frequency); LEDs may flicker at hundreds or thousands of Hz; switching to 30 fps or 60 fps "aligns the frame interval with both the power grid cycle and most LED refresh rates"; at 120 fps slow-motion "the camera is capturing frames so rapidly that it's far more likely to catch the LED switching cycle — which is why iPhone slow-motion footage shows aggressive flicker" — [GeekChamp, fix flickering video on iPhone](https://geekchamp.com/how-to-fix-flickering-video-on-iphone-in-the-us/); [BGR, why LED lights flicker on camera](https://bgr.com/2204818/why-do-led-lights-flicker-on-camera/)
- Shutter rule: 30 fps → 1/60 s, 60 fps → 1/120 s; but "regular phone cameras, for the most part, don't have shutter speed control for video" — [Joby, how to stop light flickering](https://www.joby.com/blogs/news/how-to-stop-light-flickering-in-your-videos)
- Cheap LED bulbs/panels, dimmers, fluorescent tubes and digital signage are the usual culprits; if flicker vanishes when one light is removed, avoiding that fixture is more reliable than fixing in post — [Divoom, stop LED flicker on camera](https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/stop-led-display-flickering-on-camera)

### Inferences
- India runs 50 Hz mains, so fluorescent tubes pulse at 100 Hz. Frame rates that divide evenly into 100 Hz are safest: **25 fps or 50 fps** (iPhone 15 offers 4K25/1080p25 but no 50 fps mode). 30 fps and 60 fps usually look fine on modern LED gym lighting but can roll-band under old tube lights; if you see banding at 4K30/60, switch to **4K25** (or shoot 24 fps for the cinematic flavour). For 240 fps slo-mo, shoot near a window or under the gym's steadiest LED bank, and test a 3-second clip before the working set. (The 50 Hz mapping is inference from the cited 100/120 Hz physics; no source states it for India specifically.)
- Because the iPhone silently drops below 240 fps in low light, treat the dim corners of the gym as 60 fps-only zones and save 240 fps for well-lit stations.
- Lock White Balance ON (Settings > Camera > Record Video) is worth enabling in gyms with mixed daylight + LED so colour doesn't shift mid-rep; Kartick's "off" recommendation targets walking tours, not static tripod shots.
- HDR Video (Dolby Vision) is best left OFF for CapCut/VN workflows unless you have verified the app keeps the tone mapping; the Apple page confirms the toggle path, and the known behaviour is that SDR-only pipelines make HDR footage look flat/washed. (Behaviour note is inference; the toggle path is sourced.)
- Action mode on the base iPhone 15 means 0.5x ultra-wide + 2.8K crop, so use it only for handheld walk-ins/transitions and never for the tripod-locked lift (it throws away resolution and widens limbs).

### Gaps
- Apple's user-guide pages did not render through my fetch tool (JS-driven), so the setting paths above come from Apple's search excerpts rather than the full page text; the wording was consistent across three regional variants.
- I found no Apple or lab source documenting flicker-safe frame rates specifically for Indian (50 Hz) gyms; recommendation is derived from mains-frequency physics.

## KQ4. Colour grading recipes (CapCut, VN, Lightroom) — "dark moody", "teal-orange", "clean bright", "desaturated gritty"

### Takeaway
CapCut's Adjust panel exposes brightness, contrast, saturation, exposure, temperature, tint, highlights, shadows, vibrance, sharpen, plus curves, HSL and three-way colour wheels and LUT import; VN imports .cube LUTs via Creation Kits > Filters > My Filter; Lightroom Mobile's Texture/Clarity/Dehaze run −100 to +100. ## KQ5. CapCut / VN / Edits step-by-step techniques

### Takeaway
All three apps now cover the core gym-edit toolkit: CapCut has Speed > Curve presets, auto beat markers, Track (motion tracking) for text/stickers, Freeze, Motion blur, masks for split-screen/clone, and templates; VN has Speed Curve with 6 presets plus custom points, "Music Beats"/auto-beat detection markers, keyframes and LUT import; Instagram Edits (launched April 2025) has Beat Markers, Captions with Typewriter animation, keyframes, adjustment layers, colour correction, 400+ SFX and volume ducking.

### Cited Findings

**CapCut (mobile)**

### Inferences (consolidated step-by-step cheat sheet)
- **Velocity edit (CapCut):** import 240 fps clip → Edit > Speed > Curve → pick "Hero" (slow in middle) or "Montage" → drag the lowest point to sit exactly on the beat marker → enable Smooth/optical-flow interpolation if the toggle appears for the clip → add Motion blur (Effects > Video effects > "Motion blur") to hide stutter on the fast segments.
- **Beat sync:** CapCut: add music → tap the audio track → "Beat" > Auto-generate (choose beat density) → markers appear on the timeline; cut clips so each cut lands on a marker. VN: add music → Music Beats > Auto-Beat Detection. Edits: Audio > toggle Beat Markers. Then enable snapping so clip ends magnet to markers.
- **Shake/flash on beat:** CapCut Effects > Video effects > "Shake"/"Flash" (or add a 2-frame white solid at 70% opacity on the beat); for a tighter manual version use scale keyframes 100 → 108 → 100 over 3 frames.
- **Keyframe zoom (punch-in):** tap clip → Keyframe icon at frame A (scale 100%) → move 20 frames → pinch to 115% → second keyframe auto-adds; set easing to "ease out".
- **Freeze frame:** CapCut: tap clip at the peak contraction → "Freeze" (creates a 3 s still you can shorten); VN: split then set the second segment's speed to 0 via a still-frame export; Edits: use the Freeze/duplicate-frame option or keyframe opacity over a screenshot overlay.
- **Text tracking:** CapCut Track workflow (sourced above); keep the box on a high-contrast body part (wrist wrap, barbell sleeve) rather than skin on skin.
- **Split-screen wrong vs right:** lock the tripod, film the wrong rep and right rep without moving the phone → in CapCut add the second clip as Overlay → Mask > Linear/Split, drag the edge to the centre → add "WRONG"/"RIGHT" text with ✗/✓ stickers.
- **Clone:** same as split-screen but with a vertical mask boundary where the two "yous" don't cross; feather the mask edge ~5–10%.
- **Templates:** CapCut's template library (search "gym phonk", "aura montagem") gives pre-timed velocity cuts — replace the placeholder clips with your 240 fps clips, then detach the template's music if licensing for Instagram is a concern and re-add a track from Edits' royalty-free library.

## KQ6. Gym photo angles and posing for physique photos

### Takeaway
The repeatable formula is: shoot within ~30 minutes of finishing a high-rep, short-rest set when the pump is at its visual peak; stand at a slight angle to the mirror (never square on), engage the core, hand on waist to pop the arm; choose side/window light over overhead fluorescents to reveal striations; and use a slightly elevated camera for a dominant look or a low angle for height.

### Cited Findings
- A muscle pump "looks impressive for about 30 minutes, then quickly fades over the next 1–3 hours"; 61% of surveyed respondents said their pump lasted under an hour; to maximise it, "do a few sets of 8–20 reps, keep tension on your muscles throughout those sets, and use short rest times"; hydration and carbs (fast carbs post-workout, pasta/rice the night before) help; 8 g citrulline malate and dietary nitrates are cited as pump supplements — [Outlift, How long does a muscle pump last](https://outlift.com/how-long-does-a-muscle-pump-last/)
- Pump lasts on average 2–3 hours; higher rep ranges (12–20) with 30–60 s rest produce a stronger, longer-lasting pump than heavy low-rep sets; hydration critical — [TuffWraps, how long does a pump last](https://www.tuffwraps.com/blogs/news/how-long-does-a-pump-last); [Biology Insights, gym pump duration](https://biologyinsights.com/how-long-does-a-gym-pump-last-tips-to-extend-it/)
- Mirror selfie: "Stand at a slight angle (never straight-on), relax your shoulders and tighten your core, keep your expression neutral or subtly confident, and use natural lighting near windows if possible"; angle slightly to the side "to create more depth and show off your side profile, including glutes or lats" — [GoodLife Fitness, perfect gym selfie](https://blog.goodlifefitness.com/article/how-to-take-the-perfect-gym-selfie)
- Avoid over-flexing; engage the core in every pose; hand on waist accentuates a flexed tricep or bicep; photos after the workout capture the pump — [Aaptiv, gym photo tips](https://aaptiv.com/magazine/?p=18378)
- Overhead fluorescents cast unflattering shadows; side-lighting reveals striation and texture; slightly elevated angle looks "dominant and muscular", lower angle makes you "appear taller and more imposing" — [GoodLife Fitness](https://blog.goodlifefitness.com/article/how-to-take-the-perfect-gym-selfie); [Aaptiv](https://aaptiv.com/magazine/?p=18378)
- Lightroom Mobile Texture/Clarity/Dehaze behaviour and halo check (for physique photo finishing) — [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/ee/lightroom/mobile/adjust-light-and-color/adjust-effects-in-lightroom-for-mobile.html)

### Inferences
- Schedule: finish with a 2–3-set, 15–20-rep "pump finisher" (cable flyes, lateral raises, rope pushdowns) → photos within 10–30 minutes; drink ~500 ml water in the hour before and eat carbs the night before.
- Mirror selfie done right on iPhone 15: use the rear 1x camera (not the front camera) held at chest height, phone tilted ~10° to hide the screen glare, stand 1.5–2 m from the mirror, body at 30–45° to the mirror, far-side foot back, near-side hand on waist; shoot in 48 MP HEIF Max for crop room.
- Tripod self-portraits beat mirror selfies for physique shots: tripod at 110–130 cm (upper chest) for front double-bicep/side chest, 90–100 cm for a slight-low "taller" look on quad/leg shots, 3 s timer or Apple Watch/Camera Remote. Place a single light source (window or one bright fixture) 45° to the side and slightly above; turn your torso until the shadow line runs down the centre of the abs.
- Pose set for a Reels carousel: front relaxed, front double bicep, side chest (near-side arm bent), rear lat spread (from the tripod behind), ab-and-thigh with one leg forward; 2–3 frames per pose, pick the one with the shortest exhale (vacuum).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source on optimal photo timing post-workout; the 30-minute window comes from fitness publishers citing a survey.
- No source specifically addresses iPhone 15 front vs rear camera for mirror selfies; rear-camera recommendation is an inference from resolution (48 MP main vs 12 MP front).

## KQ7. Gym filming etiquette in India (consent, busy-hour tips)

### Takeaway
I found no India-specific written policy from Cult.fit, Gold's Gym India or Anytime Fitness India on member filming, and no India-specific statute cited for gym recording; the available evidence is (a) international chain rules — film only yourself, keep others out of frame unless they agreed, don't post imagery identifying others, delete on request — that Indian gyms increasingly mirror, and (b) a January 2026 Indian viral incident (the "zero civic sense" fit-check reel) that shows Indian audiences now punish creators who treat shared space as a set. Practical etiquette is therefore: ask the floor manager once, frame tight, film off-peak, never occupy a station for more than one set-length per take.

### Cited Findings
- January 2026: Indian influencer Amulya Rattan's "zero civic sense" clip — she paused a public fit-check reel to criticise a passer-by who walked behind her — drew widespread backlash that public spaces "are for everyone, not for private filming"; other Indian creators responded with parody reels exaggerating the "reel comes first" mentality — [exchange4media, influencers spin the viral 'Zero Civic Sense' reel](https://www.exchange4media.com/industry-briefing-news/influencers-take-a-sarcastic-spin-on-the-viral-zero-civic-sense-reel-151397.html) *(via search excerpt; page returned 403 on fetch)*
