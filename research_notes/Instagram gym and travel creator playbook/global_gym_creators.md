## Key Question 1 — Who are the biggest gym / bodybuilding creators and what is each one's content formula? (Style cards)

### Takeaway
As of 2026 the gym-creator top tier is led by Chris Bumstead (~26M IG), Vladimir "Anatoly" Shmondenko (~24.5M), Ashton Hall (~18.6M), Tibo InShape (~16–19M), Sam Sulek (~7.4M), Ramon Dino (~7.2M), Noel Deyzel (~6.4M), David Laid (~4.6–6M), Joey Swoll (~5.5M), Larry Wheels (~5.3M), Leana Deeb (~4.8M), Jeff Nippard (~4.1M), Bradley Martyn (~4M) and Whitney Simmons (~4M). The formulas cluster into five repeatable archetypes: (1) raw talk-to-camera vlog (Sulek), (2) cinematic aesthetic edit (Laid, Bumstead), (3) science-educational with mixed formats (Nippard, Israetel), (4) prank / reaction / etiquette (Anatoly, Joey Swoll), and (5) humour-challenge entertainment (Jesse James West, Tibo InShape, Will Tennyson).

## Key Question 2 — Which formats get the most views/shares?

### Takeaway
In 2025–26 the breakout formats were (a) raw talk-through vlogs (Sulek), (b) prank/reveal (Anatoly), (c) timestamped day-in-the-life (Hall, ~1B combined views), (d) reaction/etiquette (Swoll), and (e) contrarian/"stall-naming" educational hooks; by contrast the plain before/after transformation opener has lost its edge and now "signals ad".

### Cited Findings
- Sulek "has exploded on YouTube despite breaking all the rules with his thumbnails, video length and low production quality… uploading one video per day is a major factor" — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/tr/blog/post/sam-sulek-breaks-youtube-rules-goes-viral) (snippet)
- Joey Swoll's reaction format "generat[es] outsized engagement well above the mega-tier norm" (4.0% vs 1.5% median) — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/joey-swoll/)

## Key Question 4 — Camera angles by body part / lift (phone + tripod)

### Takeaway
Coach/forum consensus: camera between hip and chest height, never on the floor; squat from rear-45°, deadlift from front-45°, bench from above the head at 45° (spotter's position) or pure side, overhead press from front-quarter; cinematic edits add a low 41 cm angle and tight close-ups for muscle "pop".

### Cited Findings
- "The camera should be positioned between hip and chest height, not on the floor." Squat: "rear 45-degree angle… keeps the plates out of the way and allows you to see all of the joints". Deadlift: "front 45-degree angle… head and feet in the frame at all times". Bench: "above the head of the lifter looking down at a 45 degree angle (from where the spotter would stand)… or from the side as long as you can see where the bar touches the chest and the end of the bar". Press: "front quarter… see the lockout position and the entire body at least from the knees up" — [Starting Strength forum](https://startingstrength.com/resources/forum/technique/20249-posting-technique-forum.html), [Barbell Medicine forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/camera-angle-for-main-lifts/7702)
- Fitness-class filming: phone "approximately 4 meters away… at least 1 meter off the ground" (for full-body follow-alongs) — [Fitune](https://fitune.io/post/how-to-film-high-quality-fitness-videos-with-your-phone)
- Gym tripod specs: "gym videos often need both standing eye-level and low angles (for squat depth, push-up hand position, or bench bar path)… 25–50 cm minimum height for ground-level angles, and max height of 60–70 in (150–180 cm)" — [GeekChamp](https://geekchamp.com/?p=284907)
- General: frontal, side, overhead and close-ups; manual focus/exposure/white balance; higher fps for smoother playback; face the light source; test shots first — [KraftGeek](https://kraftgeek.com/blogs/creator-inspiration/a-comprehensive-guide-to-recording-workout-videos-on-your-phone)
- Cinematic grammar: wide, tight close-ups "highlighting form and muscle engagement", slow-mo heavy lifts, over-the-shoulder — [Contra](https://contra.com/s/ZWENAm7Z-gym-and-fitness-video-editing); 120 fps for noticeable slow-mo, 240 fps for ultra-smooth — [Finchley](https://www.finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/mastering-slow-motion-video-editing-cinematic-tips-for-your-next-shoot); "dramatic side light sculpts the movement" — [Finchley](https://www.finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/mastering-slow-motion-video-editing-cinematic-tips-for-your-next-shoot)
- Ashton Hall's tripod work is often visible/unedited and he films at hip-level static tripod per scene — [The Ringer](https://theringer.com/2025/03/25/pop-culture/morning-routine-viral-video-explainer)
- 2026 cover-frame advice: capture peak motion mid-rep — [Social Boost Digital](https://socialboostdigital.com/niches/fitness-coach-social-media-strategy)
